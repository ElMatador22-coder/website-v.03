Forecast on what the buyer did.

Forecasts miss when stages move on seller activity. Here is what to count instead, and a weekly check that keeps happy ears out of the commit number.

Why forecasts miss

The forecast is built on what the seller did.

Most forecasts are a roll-up of stages, and most stages move when the seller does something, like sending the proposal or running the demo. None of that tells you whether the buyer is going to sign. That is how a pipeline full of commit deals turns into a quarter of slipped dates.

The fix is to forecast on what the buyer did. A buyer who introduced you to the economic buyer and agreed dates in writing is telling you something real. A buyer who loved the demo is telling you they loved the demo.

How SalesOgre forecasts

Scored on events, updated when they happen.

SalesOgre maps each deal against MEDDPICC from your notes, emails and calls and scores it on buyer events: who replied, who went quiet, who is missing from the room, and which commitments were made and kept.

Questions

Common questions. Straight answers.

Why are B2B sales forecasts usually inaccurate?

Because most forecasts are built on stages that move on seller activity, such as demos held and proposals sent. Those steps say little about whether the buyer will sign. Forecasts built on buyer-side evidence hold up better.

What is buyer-side evidence in a sales forecast?

Things the buyer did: replied, kept a commitment, introduced a new stakeholder, put dates in writing, engaged legal or procurement. Missed commitments and stakeholders going quiet count as evidence too.

What are happy ears in sales?

Hearing a buying signal that is not there. A great meeting or a promise to share the demo internally gets logged as progress when the buyer has not actually committed to anything.

How does SalesOgre score the forecast?

It scores each deal on buyer events mapped to MEDDPICC and advances a stage only on a buyer-side commitment. Timeline Confidence and Honest Quota scoring show what the pipeline actually is. See MEDDPICC deal scoring for the detail.
